1. What it is Contents

The Assistant is an add-on, called a plugin, for the Claude desktop app on Windows. It gives you two things.

The first is an assistant that reads for you. Every 15 minutes it reads the accounts you connect (mail, calendar, and your texts from Google Messages) and works out what needs you. Each thing goes on one private page, called your page, as a short card. You answer a card with a press or a few typed words. Your page opens in the web browser on your computer, and on your phone too if you want. At the top of it, Talk to your assistant is a box where you can ask for things in your own words.

The second is roadmaps. If you use Claude for bigger work, like a book, a renovation or a program, a roadmap is a live web page listing every step of the project and where each one stands. Claude keeps it up to date as it works. It asks you questions there, and when a piece is ready it gives you small checks to try. Your page can show your roadmaps too. A smaller tool, the questions file, keeps the questions Claude asks you in conversation from getting lost (part 5).

What it never does:

  • It never sends anything without your approval. An email or a text goes only when you press the send button on it, or when your own words to your assistant tell it to send that one. The one other way is something you approved ahead of time yourself (a standing approval, part 4), which you can pause or remove whenever you like.
  • It changes a mailbox (archive, label, mark read, move to the trash) only when you press that button on an email or ask for it in Talk to your assistant.
  • It never changes its own program by itself. A change to how it works or looks happens only after your clear go, and can be undone (part 4). Updates happen only when you say so (part 6).
  • Nothing runs in the background until you say yes at the end of setup.
  • It runs on your own computer, and what it reads stays there. When it needs to think, it uses your own Claude plan, the way a chat with Claude does.

2. Install Contents

What you need first

  1. A Windows 10 or 11 computer.
  2. The Claude desktop app, on a paid Claude plan. Install it, sign in, and open the Code tab at the top once. The Code tab is where you type to Claude about files and folders on your computer, and every command in this manual is typed there.
  3. Python. Python is a free program; the assistant is written in it. You don't have to install it ahead of time: setup checks for it first and, if it's missing, walks you through getting it (python.org/downloads, the button "Download Python install manager", install, then close and reopen Claude). It takes about five minutes.

The two lines to type

  1. In the Claude app, click the Code tab and start a session in any folder, for example your Documents. A session is one conversation with Claude, opened on one folder. Setup asks later where your assistant should live, so this folder doesn't matter yet.
  2. Type the first line below and press Enter. It tells Claude where to find the plugin.
  3. When that's done, type the second line and press Enter. It installs the plugin for every folder on your computer.
  4. Close the Claude app completely. Closing the window isn't enough: right-click the Claude icon near the clock at the bottom right of the screen and choose Quit. Then open Claude again. Claude only sees a new plugin after a restart.

Installing doesn't start anything running. Setup comes next.

Before setup: where your assistant will live

Setup asks where to put your assistant's folder. That folder holds the assistant's program and everything it keeps about you: your messages, cards, sign-ins and history. Pick a place where it can stay, because moving it later means setting it up again.

Keep it out of anything copied to the internet all the time: OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive or iCloud. Those programs upload and lock files while they work, and that gets in the way of your assistant and Claude changing their own files.

Watch out for Documents. On many Windows computers OneDrive quietly backs up the Documents folder, so a folder you make there ends up in OneDrive. To check, open Documents in File Explorer and look at the address bar at the top. If the address has OneDrive in it, your Documents is in OneDrive. Setup checks this for you and won't suggest a folder that is kept in step with one of these services.

3. Setup Contents

Setup is an interview with Claude. It asks one thing at a time, in plain words, with numbered choices (answer with the number), and takes about 30 minutes. Most of that is connecting accounts.

Start it

  1. In the Claude app's Code tab, start a session in any folder.
  2. Type /assistant:setup and press Enter.

If Python is missing, Claude walks you through installing it, then asks you to quit and reopen Claude and type /assistant:setup again. If Claude says the assistant's parts are missing, quit and reopen Claude once and try again.

Choosing your assistant's folder

Claude checks each place first and offers up to three suggestions, then "somewhere else":

  • a new folder called Assistant in your Documents, if your Documents isn't kept in step with a cloud service;
  • C:\Assistant, at the top of your C: drive, outside every cloud service;
  • the folder your session is open in, if it's empty.

For "somewhere else", open the folder you want in File Explorer, click the address bar, copy it and paste it into the chat; it's made if it doesn't exist. If the place you pick is kept in step with a cloud service, Claude warns you and suggests one outside it; it uses that place only if you choose to anyway. A folder that already has other things in it isn't used: choose an empty or new one.

Claude then says the full place back and asks you to confirm it. If it's not the folder your session is open in, open Claude for your assistant in that folder from now on. Claude then puts the program in System in that folder and everything about you in Live, and installs any free parts the program needs. That takes a minute. Nothing runs yet.

First, a look at what is already here

Before the first question, Claude looks over your computer and your Claude for anything already set up that could get in the way. It only looks; nothing changes unless you say yes. It checks for:

  • skills and plugins that do the same kind of work (an assistant, a briefing, mail, a roadmap, calendar or texts);
  • things Windows starts by itself that run a program, especially ones with such names, and ones that point at files that are gone;
  • the connections Claude already has, so setup can use them;
  • project folders in your Documents with their own instructions or rules for Claude, and instructions that name files or folders no longer on the computer;
  • something else already answering at your page's spot on this computer, and what Tailscale already shares if you use it;
  • an earlier assistant's data where this one will live, and older copies of an assistant elsewhere in your Documents.

Claude raises each thing it finds, one at a time, says what it is, how it's used and what would clash, and offers only the choices that fit:

ChoiceWhat happens
Leave it as it isNothing changes.
Remove a skill, or an older copy of an assistantIt is deleted for good.
Remove something Windows starts by itselfIt is deleted from Windows' scheduled jobs for good.
Remove a pluginYou take it off yourself; Claude shows you the line to type.
Remove earlier assistant data in the new assistant's placeIt is deleted for good, and a fresh, empty one is made.
Move your page to a free spotYour page uses the next free spot on this computer.
Change it yourself (a project's rules or instructions)Claude shows you exactly what to change. It never edits another project's files.

Before any remove, and before moving the page, Claude says in one line what will happen and asks "Go ahead? 1 yes, 2 no." What was found and decided is remembered; if you stop part way, /assistant:setup raises only what's still undecided. If your assistant's own folder turns out to be in a cloud-synced place, the only choice is to carry on for now or to stop and move it: type /assistant:uninstall (it keeps your data), then /assistant:setup in a folder outside the service.

Switching from an older setup

Moving an assistant you already run onto the plugin

If an earlier assistant keeps its program and everything it knows together in one folder (a System folder and a Live folder side by side), you don't have to start again. /assistant:adopt moves it onto the plugin. Its cards, rules, people, settings, sign-ins and page password are kept. Your page is away for a few minutes, and nothing changes until you give a clear go.

  1. Close any File Explorer window that has the old folder open. Windows refuses to move a folder that's open.
  2. Install the plugin (part 2), open a Code tab session in your old assistant's folder, and type /assistant:adopt.
  3. Claude confirms which folder holds your assistant, then asks where it should live, the way setup does, with two differences: it can stay where it is (nothing is moved, only its program is replaced, and the old program is saved first as the way back), and if the old folder holds other things too, Claude suggests a new folder called Assistant inside it first, because staying put would make that whole folder your assistant's.
  4. Claude shows the plan: how many parts of the program are the same as the plugin's, how many the plugin adds, how many are yours and stay, and which differ. For each one that differs it says what's different and asks: 1 keep yours, 2 take the plugin's, 3 combine both.
  5. Claude tells you how many open cards your page has now, so you can check the same number is there afterwards.
  6. Claude says it all back and asks "Shall I make the switch now? 1 yes, go, 2 not now." Only a clear yes goes ahead.
  7. The switch stops the old assistant, moves everything it knows into the new folder, lays down the plugin's program, reads once to check, and starts the new one in the background. If any step fails, everything goes back as it was and the old one starts again.
  8. Claude then checks that the background job is on, that your page answers (open it and look), that the check read went well, and that the number of open cards is the same. If you use your page on your phone, open it there too: it keeps its address.

The old program is kept, unused, as the way back. To undo the move, type /assistant:adopt again and say "way back". From now on, open your sessions about the assistant in its folder, and use /assistant:update for updates.

Starting fresh instead

  1. Keep a copy of anything you want from the old assistant. Setup's first look deletes nothing on its own; an old copy is deleted only when you choose remove for it.
  2. Run /assistant:setup. Its first look finds the old assistant's scheduled parts, its folders, older skills and the old Roadmap plugin, and asks about each.
  3. For each scheduled part of the old assistant, choose remove: it's deleted from Windows' scheduled jobs. One already running keeps going until the computer restarts, so restart the computer once setup is finished.
  4. If the old assistant's page still answers at the spot the new page wants, choose to move your page to a free spot.
  5. Tailscale is left as it is. Your new page takes a public spot that isn't in use, so its address may end in a different number from the old one; the old address stops working once the old assistant is off.
  6. The old Roadmap plugin, roadmap@roadmap-plugin, has to come off, or its blocks and the assistant's both run in the same project. If it was installed for one project, open a Code tab session in that project's folder and type the first line below (in every project it was on). If it was installed for all of Claude, type the second line in any session. Then quit and reopen Claude.
  7. Existing roadmaps keep their pages, links and everything on them. Their commands are now /assistant:roadmap-setup, /assistant:roadmap-update and /assistant:roadmap-start-writer. To keep one going, type /assistant:roadmap-update once in that project's main session. To stop or remove one, see part 5.

/plugin uninstall roadmap@roadmap-plugin

Connecting accounts

You do the clicking in your own browser. Claude tells you each click and waits for you to say "done". It never types your password. When a website gives you a long secret key, a small window opens on your computer and you paste the key there, never into the chat.

Your assistant has its own connection for each kind of account. It takes longer to set up once (Google about 15 minutes, Microsoft about 10); after that, reading costs nothing against your Claude plan. One Google registration covers as many Gmail and Google work accounts as you like.

  • Gmail, and Google work mail: register your own free connection with Google once, then each account is one sign-in of about a minute. Google warns that it hasn't verified the app. That's expected, since the app is your own.
  • Google Calendar: uses the same Google registration. Claude then asks which of your calendars to show under Today on your page.
  • Microsoft work or school mail: about 10 minutes. Some employers block outside apps, and then you need their IT person. A personal outlook.com or hotmail.com mailbox is not covered.
  • Texts from Google Messages: signed in from your page after it is switched on (see below).

After each account, Claude reads it once to check it works and tells you the result. If it didn't work, you fix it together or leave it for later. Claude keeps asking "Another account?" until you say that's all.

Gmail, Google work mail and Google Calendar

Google only hands mail to a program registered with it. You register your own, for free; nobody else can use it. Part A is done once (about 15 minutes); part B is one sign-in per mailbox or calendar (about a minute). Screen names below follow Google's own help pages where they name them; if your screen differs, follow the screen.

Part A: register once

  1. Open console.cloud.google.com and sign in with your Google account. If it asks you to agree to terms, agree.
  2. Make a project: open the menu at the top left, then "IAM & Admin", then "Create a Project". Name it "My assistant" and click "Create". When it is made, make sure it is the project picked at the top of the page.
  3. In the search bar at the top type Gmail API, click "Gmail API" in the results, then click "Enable". Do the same for "Google Calendar API" (needed for calendars).
  4. Open the menu, then "Google Auth platform", then "Branding". If it says "Google Auth platform not configured yet", click "Get Started".
  5. On "App Information": App name "My assistant"; User support email: pick your own address. Click "Next".
  6. On "Audience": choose "External" and click "Next". (If every account you will connect is in one Google work organization and you see "Internal", you may choose that instead and skip steps 9 and 10.)
  7. On "Contact Information": your email address, "Next".
  8. On "Finish": tick that you agree to the Google API Services User Data Policy, click "Continue", then "Create".
  9. In the left menu click "Audience". Under "Test users" click "Add users", add every Google address you will connect, and click "Save".
  10. Still on "Audience", click "Publish app" and confirm, so the status says "In production". This matters: left in testing, Google ends your sign-in every 7 days.
  11. In the left menu click "Clients", then "Create Client". Application type: "Desktop app". Name: "My assistant". Leave the box "Use this client for an AI-powered agent" unticked. Click "Create".
  12. A box shows "Client ID" and "Client secret". Google shows the secret only this once, so keep that box open.
  13. Tell Claude the "Client ID" (it ends in .apps.googleusercontent.com; it is not secret). Claude connects it, and a small window opens for the "Client secret": paste it there, not in the chat. If the box was closed and the secret lost: on the client's page click "Add Secret" and use the new one.

Part B: each mailbox or calendar

  1. Your browser opens Google's own sign-in page. Pick the account you want.
  2. Google warns that it has not verified this app. That is expected: the app is your own. Click "Advanced", then "Go to My assistant (unsafe)".
  3. Tick every box Google lists (read mail, send mail, or see calendars) and click "Continue". Your password goes only to Google; your assistant never sees it.
  4. The browser says "Signed in." Close that tab. Claude tells you whether the check read the account.
  5. For more Gmail or Google work accounts, repeat 14 to 17 for each. Each must be in the test users from step 9. Connect a calendar the same way.

If something goes wrong

  • "Access blocked: My assistant has not completed the Google verification process": the address is not in the test users (step 9), or the app is not published (step 10).
  • "Access blocked: admin policy" on a work account: the organization limits outside apps. Its Google administrator must allow your Client ID.
  • Google may limit an unverified app to 100 users in its whole life. A personal assistant has one user, so this does not matter.
  • Later the page says "sign this account in again": type /assistant:change accounts and choose to sign one in again.

Microsoft work or school mail

This covers a mailbox at work or school that runs on Microsoft 365. You register your own connection with Microsoft, for free, once (about 10 minutes). Your company may not let staff do this; step 2 shows that, and then your IT person can do steps 2 to 8 for you and give you the two codes from step 5.

Register once

  1. Open entra.microsoft.com and sign in with your work email.
  2. In the left menu click "Entra ID", then "App registrations", then "New registration" at the top. If it says you do not have access, your company blocks this.
  3. Name: "My assistant". Under "Supported account types" leave the first choice, "Single tenant only".
  4. Click "Register".
  5. You are on the app's "Overview". Tell Claude both the "Application (client) ID" and the "Directory (tenant) ID"; they are not secret.
  6. In the left menu click "Authentication", then "Add a platform", then "Mobile and desktop applications". Tick or type the address http://localhost and click "Configure".
  7. Still under "Authentication", find "Allow public client flows" (under "Advanced settings", or on a "Settings" tab in the newer layout) and set it to "Yes". Click "Save".
  8. In the left menu click "API permissions", then "Add a permission", then "Microsoft Graph", then "Delegated permissions". Under "Select permissions" search Mail and tick "Mail.ReadWrite" and "Mail.Send"; search offline and tick "offline_access". "User.Read" is already there. Click "Add permissions".
  9. Claude connects it.

Each mailbox

  1. Your browser opens Microsoft's own sign-in page. Sign in with the mailbox and click "Accept" on the permissions page. Your password goes only to Microsoft.
  2. The browser says "Signed in." Close that tab. Claude tells you whether the check read the mailbox.

If something goes wrong

  • "Need admin approval": give your administrator the "Application (client) ID" and ask them to grant consent for Mail.ReadWrite, Mail.Send, User.Read and offline_access.
  • Later the page says "sign this account in again" (often after a password change): type /assistant:change accounts and choose to sign one in again.

Texts from Google Messages

Your assistant links to Google Messages itself, the way Messages for web does. It reads your texts, sent and received, one-to-one and group, RCS chats included, and sends a text only when you press Send on your page. This link is made from your page, so it comes after you switch your assistant on.

Open the Texts section of your page and press Sign in to Google Messages. The page shows the steps. They use a private window in Firefox: you open Google's sign-in address there, sign in with the Google account your phone's Google Messages uses, copy one line from the browser's developer panel, paste it into the box on your page and press Hand over the sign-in. Then an emoji shows on the page; tap the same one in Google Messages on your phone, and close the private window without signing out of Google. The last week of texts is read in once right after you sign in; after that new texts arrive as they come.

If texts stop arriving, the Texts section and the Errors box say so: texts are disconnected (sign in again the same way), your phone is not answering (check it is on and has signal or Wi-Fi), texts are not connected since a time, or the texts link on this computer has not checked in since a time. When it is connected they say nothing.

Only one place at a time can hold Google Messages' session. If you open Messages for web in a browser, the setting Google Messages in a browser (Settings, Inbox, Sources) decides: Reconnect right away (the starting choice) takes it back at once, and Let the browser keep it until the next check pauses texts here (the Texts section says Paused while Messages is open in your browser) until the next check takes the session back and reads in what came meanwhile. Google does not tell your assistant when the browser tab is closed, which is why the check does it. To have it back sooner, press Take it back now in the Texts section.

Introducing other projects

If you use Claude for other work, a project can send you cards. For each project Claude asks:

  1. Which project, and roughly where its folder is. Claude finds it and asks you to confirm.
  2. What happening there should come to you as a card, in your words (for example "a quote is ready for me to check").
  3. What should go back to the project when you answer. "Nothing" is a fine answer.

Then Claude asks for a separate yes before each change it would make inside that project, and makes none without one:

  • The introduction: a small hidden folder in that project holding the words you agreed, and a tray where that project's Claude leaves a note for you each time.
  • One line at the end of that project's instructions for Claude, so its Claude reads the introduction every time. Claude shows you the line first.
  • Its roadmap on your page, if it has one or you want one. If it has none, make one later with /assistant:roadmap-setup in that project (part 5).

Introduce, change or remove a project at any time with /assistant:introduce, or with Start beside Introduce a project in Settings, Tools on your page.

How far back to look

  1. A card asks how far back to look. One year is the default; you can pick another span or type your own.
  2. This is fast and fairly cheap. Nothing from the past is read or made into cards: your past messages are only cataloged (who each was from, the subject, the date), so that when something new comes in your assistant can look back for the same sender or subject and give it context.
  3. This step also builds your voice guide from a few of your sent emails, so drafts sound like you.
  4. Until the catalog is built, no new messages come in, so the first ones are judged with context. A card shows how far it has got.

Reaching your page from your phone

Your page always lives on your computer and is locked with a password you choose. You pick one of two options.

  • Only on this computer. It opens in a browser on that computer, at an address starting http://127.0.0.1:.
  • From anywhere. It also opens on your phone or another computer, through Tailscale, a free program (free for personal use) that gives your computer its own private, encrypted address. You install it and sign in yourself; your assistant never does that for you. You get an address starting https://.

From anywhere, the page only opens while your computer is on and awake. Anyone with the address sees only the sign-in, and wrong passwords from outside are limited.

Setting up Tailscale

Tailscale goes on your computer only. Nothing needs installing on your phone: the address opens in any web browser. This step has to be done at the computer.

  1. Open tailscale.com/download/windows, click the download, run it, and sign in when it asks (with Google, Microsoft or another account).
  2. Tell Claude when Tailscale says it is connected. Claude checks that Tailscale is ready.
  3. Claude switches your page on for outside. The first time, your browser may open a Tailscale page asking you to approve turning on its sharing feature (called "Funnel") for this computer. Approve it there, then tell Claude, and Claude tries again.
  4. Claude checks it and gives you your page's address, starting https://. Send it to your phone, open it there, and sign in with your page's password.

Good to know:

  • The first time, the address can take up to 10 minutes to start working. Claude can check again.
  • If Tailscale is already used on this computer for something else, that is left as it is; the page takes a free spot, and its address may end in :8443 or :10000.
  • "Tailscale is not on this computer yet" or "not signed in": do step 1 again, then step 2.
  • No free spot: Tailscale here already uses all three of its public spots for other things. The page stays on this computer only.

To find your page's address again, open Settings, then Tools: the entry Page location shows where it lives now. Or at your computer type /assistant:change page and choose to check that it can be reached. Setup also gives you the address at the very end; bookmark it on your phone.

Putting your page on your phone's home screen

Once your page opens on your phone (this needs the https:// address, not the computer-only one), you can give it an icon on your home screen so it opens like an app. In Settings, Tools, Add to your phone's home screen does it: on a phone whose browser offers it, Add it now brings up the phone's own install prompt; otherwise Show me how shows the steps. They are:

  • Android, in Chrome: tap the three dots at the top right, then Add to Home screen (or Install app), then Add or Install.
  • iPhone, in Safari: tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.

The icon opens the same page with the same password, and like any way of opening it from your phone it only works while your computer is on and awake.

What it may send

Setup asks one question: may your assistant send email for you at all, when you approve it? If you say no, nothing can ever be sent. If you say yes, an email only goes when you press its send button on your page, or when it's a kind you've approved ahead of time (see "Standing approvals", part 4). Later, once it sees you approve the same kind of email a few times, it offers to send that kind without asking, and only if you say yes.

Switching it on, and your password

At the end, Claude asks whether to switch on your assistant's one hidden background job. It starts when you sign in to Windows, serves your page, answers your presses at once, and reads your accounts every 15 minutes (you can set that to 30 minutes or an hour in Settings, Inbox, Sources, Check every). No window ever pops up, it needs no administrator rights, and nothing else runs.

If you say yes:

  1. Your page opens in your browser.
  2. Choose its password there, 10 characters or more. Type it only on that page, never in the chat with Claude.
  3. Tell Claude when you can see your page.

Claude finishes by telling you what's set up and your page's address. Keep it: bookmark it on your computer and, if you chose from anywhere, on your phone. You can change the password later in Settings, General, Sign-in.

If you say "not yet", nothing runs. Type /assistant:change switch-on when you're ready.

Stopping and picking up later

You can stop at any time: type "stop", or just close the window. Everything you've answered is already saved. To carry on, type /assistant:setup again. Claude says "Welcome back", tells you which steps are done, and picks up where you left off.

Changing an answer

During setup, just say so at any question, for example "actually my areas are Work, Family, Church". Claude changes it, says "Changed: ...", and goes back to where you were.

After setup:

  • Your name, areas, what matters, junk, filters and how the page looks: change them in Settings on your page (part 4).
  • Accounts, where the page can be opened, sending, reading further back and switching on: type /assistant:change in the Code tab, in your assistant's folder, or press Start beside Change in Settings, Tools. Add a word to go straight to one part: /assistant:change accounts, page, sending, read-back or switch-on. Settings, Tools also lists Accounts, Sending email and Page location, each with Show the command and a Copy button to paste into Claude on your computer. Set up again there goes over part of your setup again without starting over.
  • Projects: type /assistant:introduce.

4. Your page Contents

Your page is the assistant's page of cards: everything that needs you, in one place. It updates by itself as new things arrive, without disturbing a card you are typing in. Every part folds and opens when you press its heading, and the page remembers what you folded on that device. Folding a part while you are further down in it brings its heading back up, just under the top bar.

The top of the page

  • The title line is pinned to the top of the screen. It holds the page's title, Settings (it reads Close settings while Settings is open) and today's date and time.
  • Search everything on this page shows only the cards whose words match. The list beside it (Everything) narrows to one group or one area. Show everything clears both, and a line says how many of the cards are showing.
  • Talk to your assistant is the box "Ask or tell your assistant anything", with Send. Ask about your own mail, calendar, texts and cards, or tell it something. Enter sends; Shift and Enter starts a new line (3,000 characters at most). While it works the box says "Sending..." or "Working on it...". If no answer comes within about four minutes, it says your words were saved and to ask again. If your computer is off or not listening, it says it will answer when the computer is back. Older talk folds under Earlier talk; on a phone the newest exchange folds under Last talk. Clear the talk hides what was said on this device (nothing is deleted), with an Undo. Open in the Claude app (not shown on a phone) opens a Claude session in the assistant's folder that shares this conversation: what you say there shows here marked "In the Claude app", and what you say here is read there. The box can do what the page's buttons do, but only when your own words ask for it, and it cannot change a setting; only you can, in Settings. A change to how your assistant works, or a rule, asked here becomes its own card (part 4, later).
  • Errors shows only when there is something to read, for example "Sync is off", "Sync is behind", "Today's limit on AI runs is reached", "Your computer is not listening", or a line about Google Messages. It shows only what is true now, so a later reading that worked clears an earlier trouble. The footer at the very bottom says "All is well" with when your accounts were last gathered, or points to Errors.

When a newer version of the page is ready, a strip says so and loads it by itself once you are not typing, or at once with Load it now.

The sections

Your cards are one part, Cards, in the main column: one list, with no headings or folds inside it. Buttons across its top narrow it, each with how many: All, Needs you (the one it opens on), Waiting on others, Someday, FYI, Done and Hidden. Beside them are Group by kind (On or Off) and Sort by, each with an (i) that says what it does. When you go to a card from a search or the chat, Cards shows All so the card is in view.

Sort by offers Due date (every card with a due date first, the soonest first, then important, then the people you are most in touch with, then the oldest; the default), Newest first and Oldest first (by the date at the top right of each card), Waiting longest (the card that has sat longest where it is), Importance (most important first, then the newest) and Recently active (the latest message first). With Group by kind on, the kinds stay together (the first being the one you set in Settings, under Appearance, Card order) and Sort by orders the cards within each kind; with it off, every card the button shows is one list, by Sort by alone. Hidden is always last. Your choice is saved at once and kept for every device.

  • Needs you: everything that needs a decision, an answer or something only you can do. Quick questions come after the others, as Needs you, quick answers, and show "The best guess" in place of a recommendation. How often you are in touch with someone counts your real two-way mail, texts and other messages, the last 90 days counting most; mailing lists, automatic mail and junk do not count. It is one input: your own rules, junk lists and how strict the filter is set always come first.
  • Waiting on others: things you or someone else are already moving. A card says who it waits on and the day it is looked at again.
  • Someday: cards you snoozed with no date, each with Bring it back and Done.
  • FYI: cards that need nothing from you. Each has Done, and Done, all closes every card shown there.
  • Done: what you pressed or answered, from the press until it is settled; cards your assistant closed without anything being done, under Done by your assistant, no action taken with the reason (one joined into another card says which); cards something was done on in the last days (a setting, Keep Done cards for), each with Bring it back; and Done under your approvals, one row for each use of a standing approval.
  • Hidden: the messages kept off your page (below).

The side column holds Today: your calendar for today and the coming days, with Choose calendars (which opens that part of Settings). Roadmaps shows your projects' roadmaps, only when you have some (part 5). Email, Texts and Calendar follow below the cards, and Change history is part of Settings, Tools. The last card in Cards is "Hidden today: N." with See them, or "Nothing was hidden today."

In Settings, under Appearance, you can hide sections, rename them and drag them to another place (Settings says how). A section holding something marked important always shows.

A card and its parts

The top right of every card shows the date and time it is from: when the first message behind it came in, or, for a card no message made, when it was made. When you answer a card by typing or with one of its question's choice buttons, the card's date becomes the time you answered. A closed card shows its title, the recommendation ("Recommended: ..."), why it is on your page in two sentences at most, every question it asks, one row of buttons, and one box, Ask or tell the assistant about this card. Beside the title are only three tags: Due (or Due today, Was due), Important and New (new since you last looked on this device). A card with several questions numbers them, and each has its own buttons: its recommended answer first, its other answers, and Other.

Press the title to open the card, and again to close it. One card is open at a time: opening one closes any other. An open card shows three sections at once: Context (what happened, with every message, paper and picture it rests on written in as a link; it ends with its status and age, and says if it came back or whom it waits on), The draft (only when there is one) and Conversation (everything done and said on the card, oldest first). After each reply, the card's front follows the conversation: the recommendation, the short line under it and the question with its buttons are rewritten to where things stand now, while the full detail stays in Conversation.

A link opens the thing itself in a window of its own, the same window each time and brought to the front, while the page and card stay where they were: a PDF as the PDF, a picture as the picture, an email as the email. On a phone it opens in a new tab; where the browser blocks the window, it opens over the page with Close back to the card. A PDF or picture opened from an email shows Download, Back to the email and Close. Links that leave the page (a sender's unsubscribe page, a full roadmap, this manual) open in that same window too. Papers show the words the assistant read from them, and a scanned paper or photo is read page by page; a paper not read yet says so and asks you to look at the original before you answer.

Each card has its own conversation. What you type on a card stays with that card and does not show in Talk to your assistant. "This card" in the top box means the open card; with none open, it asks which you mean, and a card you name in words is found and asked about. What you say about a card in the top box is added to that card too.

When you answer a card, or it leaves the page while you are looking, it fades out and the cards below slide up; a new card fades in. With reduced motion set on your device, cards change without moving.

A card waiting on someone else says who, the day it is looked at again and what is recommended then. If nothing comes by that day, it comes back to you and names that person. When two cards turn out to be the same matter they are joined into one, keeping your answers from both; the other closes with "Joined into" and the card's number. A card you marked Done stays done.

Typed answers and the thinking line

(or "... about N things you wrote."), with Undo, or Undo the last one when several are waiting (not for the top box). After a press that settles a card, the card leaves its place and shows a line with what you pressed, then "Saved", "Working on it", then the result, with its own Undo. The writing screen

When an email your assistant writes needs someone's address, it looks for it first: in the card, its emails, the rest of your gathered mail and your contacts, including someone who wrote once and never heard back. It uses the address it finds and says where it came from. Only when the address is truly nowhere, or more than one person on file has that name, does it ask you.

Reply, Check and send and Edit on a draft open it. It has Type (when the email answers or passes on another one: New, Reply, Reply all or Forward; switching it fills in who it goes to, the subject and the original's files again, and keeps your words), From (every account you can send from; a reply starts as the address the original came to and goes out only from it), To (suggests names and addresses from your contacts as you type; put commas between several), Copy to, Blind copy, Subject and Message. A reply stays in its conversation; the original is folded under the message. A line says how many characters are left (one email here holds about 3,500). At most 20 recipients.

Attachments: each file shows its name and size with Remove; Add the original's attachments puts back any you removed; Add a file from your computer (15 MB at most); Add from another email finds any email by words, sender or subject, and you tick its files and press Add the ticked files.

Check and send shows exactly what will go: the type, From, To, Copy to, Blind copy, the attachment names, the subject, the message and the original. Send now sends it at once, from that account, to exactly those people; if the email changed since you saw it (the assistant wrote it again) or your computer is not listening, nothing is sent and the page says why. Back to editing returns; Close keeps your words as a draft; Throw it away drops them. The assistant never sends an email by itself.

Your edits are kept in the browser you typed them in. They never quietly change who an email goes to: if your edit has other recipients than the draft, the email says "Changed on this device" and shows both, with Use the draft's recipients; if the assistant wrote the draft again after your edit, your older edit is dropped and the new draft shows. If sending is off, or no connected account can send, the page says how to turn it on (type /assistant:change sending or /assistant:change accounts in Claude on your computer).

Email

The Email part shows your mail from every address your assistant reads, in one list, newest first, in a box as tall as Texts whose list and open email scroll inside it. Unread emails are in bold; a clip shows how many files one has. The bar at the top has an address list (All addresses or one), a view list (Inbox, Unread, Starred, Snoozed, Sent, Drafts, Junk, All mail; with unread counts), a search box with Search, New email and Refresh. Each conversation is one row with its newest line and a count. Older shows the next 50.

Search looks through every email your assistant has read, by words, subject, sender or recipients. Also search the mail services then asks each mailbox itself, which finds older mail too. In the box, from:, to:, has:attachment, before:2026-11-01, after:2026-10-01 and in:sent (or a folder name) narrow the list, with plain words alongside. Refresh shows the stored mail at once and asks your assistant to pull every mailbox right away.

Mailboxes and folders. The column on the left lists each address with its folders (Outlook) or labels (Gmail) and their unread counts; press a folder to list its mail (mail in an Inbox subfolder is listed under that folder, not in the Inbox). Press an address's name to fold its folders shut or open them; it changes nothing in the list of emails. Drag an address by its dotted handle to put your mailboxes in your own order (keyboard: Space on the handle, Up and Down, Space again); on a computer, drag the line between the column and the emails (or press Left and Right on it) to change the column's width. Mail you change elsewhere (read, moved, archived, deleted, labelled, on your phone or another computer) is followed here.

Opening an email shows its whole conversation, oldest first, with labels and attachments. The one you pressed shows at once with "Opening..." while the rest comes. Opening an unread email marks it read in the mailbox; untick Opening marks read in Settings, under Email, to stop that (kept on that device). Back to the list returns; Pop out (computers) opens that email with its buttons in a small window of its own, holding only that email and any draft you start from it. A PDF or picture attachment opens in the pop-up window with Download, Back to the email and Close; other files download.

An email shows as it was sent: its formatting, colours and pictures, in a frame that can run nothing, on a white background even when your page is dark; every link opens in a new tab. Loading pictures from the internet can tell the sender you opened it, so Settings, under Appearance, has Pictures from the internet in emails: Show (the start) or Hide. With Hide, "Pictures from the internet are hidden." sits above an email that has them, and Show pictures loads them for that email. A plain-text email shows as text. Your assistant itself reads only the plain text.

Organizing mail. On an open email: Reply, Reply all, Forward, Archive, Delete (moves it to that mailbox's trash after asking once, never for good), Junk, Unsubscribe (when the sender offers it: one press, or "opens their page", or "sends an email" after you confirm), Label (pick a label or type a new name, then Add label; a label's Remove label takes it off), Star or Remove star (Gmail's Starred and Outlook's flag, kept the same both ways), Move (to a folder or label you pick), Snooze (later today, tomorrow 8:00 AM, next week Monday 8:00 AM, or a date; at that time it returns to the inbox as unread, and Bring back now does it at once) and Mark read or Mark unread. Each happens once, on your press, in the mailbox itself. Archive, Delete and Junk show an Undo line. The tick box beside each row (and Select all shown) lets you Archive, Delete (asks once), Junk, Unsubscribe, Mark read, Mark unread, Snooze, Star, Unstar or Move (one mailbox at a time) several conversations at once; Clear unticks them. The Drafts view is read only: drafts made in your mail service can be read here, not changed. Talk to your assistant can do the same things, and also make, rename, move or delete folders and labels (a folder that still holds mail is deleted only after it tells you what is in it and you say yes).

Archive, Delete, Label and Mark read need your permission for the assistant to change mail. A mailbox connected before this was signed in for reading and sending only: until you sign it in again, an email says its sign-in does not allow those and the buttons are not shown. To allow it, open Setup, pick the mailbox and press Sign in, ticking every box. For a Microsoft work mailbox the registration needs "Mail.ReadWrite" first (your IT person may have to grant it).

Writing and sending. New email at the top, and Reply, Reply all and Forward on an open email, ask your assistant to start a draft; it appears in Drafts (below) and opens in the writing screen. Your signature (one for each sending account) is added to the draft; you set it by telling Talk to your assistant. Email's header says "N emails ready to send" with Drafts (Hide drafts to close): the emails Talk to your assistant wrote for you and replies you started and left. A new one opens the list by itself. Each shows its type, From, To, Copy to, Blind copy, attachments, subject, message and why it was written, with three buttons. Send now sends it at once from the account shown, exactly as shown, after your own press only (the same Send now as in the writing screen); the page then says "Sent from ..." or why it was not sent. Edit opens the writing screen. Discard asks once, then takes it off the page unsent. When several were written together, Send all N first lists exactly what will go, and Yes, send all N sends them. Nothing goes out until you press one of these, or tell Talk to your assistant to send one.

Junk cards. See "The buttons" for the junk question card. Mail from senders on your junk list is kept off your page (Hidden, below).

Reload all email history, in Settings, Tools, reads the whole history of every Gmail and Microsoft mailbox again after one confirm. Old mail it brings in is kept as history and raises no cards. Mail already stored keeps its place; nothing is deleted.

Texts

The Texts part is your texts in one place, laid out like Google Messages on the web: a list on the left (a face or initials, bold name when unread, a one-line snippet, a short time, and a pin that keeps a conversation at the top on this device) and the conversation on the right, each scrolling by itself, in a panel about 70% of the window tall. Names come from your contacts; a person's phone-backup texts and Google Messages texts are one conversation, and a group is named by its people. The bar has a search box with Search (a word in any text, or a name), New text and Refresh. Press a conversation to read it: it opens at the newest text, yours on the right in the accent colour and theirs on the left in grey, and opening it marks it read. Older texts load as you scroll up, or with Older. Texts that arrive while you read do not move what you are looking at; a small "new messages" tag takes you down. On a phone the list and the conversation take turns.

Pictures. A picture in a text shows in its conversation; press it to open it full size. Pictures come in at full size; when only the small copy is ready it shows first and is replaced when your phone sends the full one. Another kind of file is offered to save. Rules you teach it

There are four ways to teach the filter and the way cards are written:

  1. Not needed on a card: just this one, or everything from this sender (your junk list).
  2. Not needed, then Other, saying what you did not need. Your assistant proposes a rule in plain words on a card titled "New rule: ...". Save this rule saves it; Done drops it. To reword it, write in the card's box; it is rewritten in your words and is still not in force until you save it.
  3. Words that read as a standing rule, typed in a card's box or in the box at the top: "from now on ...", "always ...", or a correction such as "you should have said where" or "I don't need to see these, this is on autopay". Nothing is saved at once. The rule is proposed in one sentence with four buttons (below).
  4. Settings, Inbox, Filters, Rules. Each rule is one plain sentence (8 to 300 characters) under Show less, Always show or How cards are written. Under each is a line on why it is there (from what you pressed or said when it was made, or what the assistant read from it; old rules may have none), which you can change too; a rule card shows the same line under the rule. You can edit it, set it On or Paused, or press Remove twice ("Press again to remove"). To add one, type it and press Add a rule, then Save. Two rules that pull against each other are saved with a warning line. Rules you approve from a card or from a correction are listed here too.

Standing approvals

A standing approval lets your assistant do one kind of thing in one area without asking each time. Nothing is done for you unless you made one. There are three kinds: marking cards Done that need nothing, marking waiting cards Done that come back with no reply, and sending short replies to one person (below).

  • How one gets made. Once you have done the same kind of thing in the same area several times (5 unless you change Suggest approvals after to 3, 10 or Never), a card tagged Standing approval offers it. At your computer, /assistant:change, choice 6, talks it through instead (the other choices are accounts, page, sending, reading further back and switching on; 7 undoes a change):

  1. Say what you want in your own words.
  2. Claude asks one question at a time until it is exactly clear: what you see now, what you want, when it applies, what it must not affect.
  3. Claude checks it against the assistant's rules and program. If it would break a rule, it says so and offers a way round. If it is really one of your own settings or rules, it does it that way and the program is not changed.

  6. At your computer it asks "Do you see it working? 1 yes, 2 no." On no, everything goes back.

What a roadmap is for

When Claude does a bigger project for you, often with helpers doing parts of the work, the hard part is knowing where things really stand. A roadmap is a private web page on claude.ai that lists every step of the project and where each one stands, and Claude keeps it true while it works. You read it instead of asking. You also use it to try finished work, answer the questions the work raises and write notes. It fits any kind of project; a step can be a chapter, a room, a filing or a feature.

Each row is named as the name you already use for that part of the work, a colon, then what is true once it is finished, for example "Kitchen: cabinets hung" or "Chapter 3: draft finished".

Setting one up in a project

  1. In the Claude app's Code tab, start a session in the project's folder. This session becomes the project's main session.
  2. Type /assistant:roadmap-setup and press Enter.
  3. Claude asks what to call the roadmap (it suggests the folder's name plus "Roadmap") and which kind it is, stepped or running (see "Stepped and running roadmaps").
  4. Approve the steps as Claude asks. It publishes your new, empty page, starts a second session called Roadmap writer beside it, and gives you the page's link.
  5. Tell Claude your plan. The steps appear on the page.

From then on, work with the main session as usual and open the page whenever you want to see where things stand. To share it, use the page's Share menu. People you share it with can read it; only you and editors can mark checks and answer questions.

The main session and the Roadmap writer, in plain words

  • The main session is the Claude you talk to. It plans with you, directs the work, reads the roadmap, and tells the writer every change.
  • The Roadmap writer is a second Claude session. Its only job is writing the roadmap from what the main session tells it, and it never talks to you.
  • Helpers are other sessions doing parts of the work. They never write the roadmap; they report to the main session. By default each helper uses the cheapest model that does its job well; you can set them to always use the strongest (see "Settings on the roadmap page").
  • The reminders keep it honest without stopping work. If the main session starts or messages a helper before telling the writer what you said, or a helper's report has not been passed on, its next message starts with a reminder to send the writer what it missed. Nothing is refused or held for this. Claude can't switch the reminders off. Only you can.

What the plugin locks. In a project with a roadmap, no Claude session or helper can change the roadmap's own safety files: the project's roadmap records and logbook (.claude oadmap), its settings files and hook scripts (.claude\settings*.json, .claude\hooks), and the plugin's five safeguard scripts, in the plugin's folder and its working copy: the lock itself, the roadmap blocks, the copy the lock loads, the list that turns the hooks on, and setup. Everything else in the plugin can be edited and committed as usual. Switching the roadmap off or on is yours alone.

If you start a fresh session and want it to be the main one, type /assistant:roadmap-setup in it. That keeps your page and only switches the session. If the Roadmap writer session is closed, type /assistant:roadmap-start-writer in the main session. When the project's setup is unfinished because no writer is recorded, the main session is reminded of this at each of your messages.

A project that writes its own roadmap. You can switch a project so the main session writes the roadmap itself, with no Roadmap writer session. It writes each change in one short step in its own turn, says nothing about it in the chat, and tells you once, in one line, only when a write fails (what did not get recorded). Helpers still never write the roadmap: the plugin refuses their writes, and the main session writes what their reports mean in the turn it gets them. The reminders above give way to one check at the end of each reply: if a helper's finished report is not on the roadmap yet (a note that a helper only finished a turn, such as "still running", is not one), the reply is held once, naming it, so Claude writes it; a reply that owes nothing, such as a plain answer, is never held. Other work the roadmap does not show yet (a helper just started) is a reminder at the start of the next message, not a hold, and so is a roadmap written while the page's copy was not brought up to date after it, where your assistant's page shows the roadmap whole. That copy is kept by handing over only the rows just written, so a big roadmap costs no more to keep than a small one. If it is ever missing or damaged, Claude says so once and leaves it; rebuilding it is a large job done only when you ask.

Only you switch this, in your own terminal (not in Claude, which is refused), in the project's folder. Replace <project folder> with the project's real folder; the roadmap page's Settings shows the exact line with Copy.

Claude checks everything it can itself first (the code, a test page or test cards, never pressing anything on your real data and never sending), and work it fully checked goes straight to Done, confirmed. A row that's ready for your look carries only the checks Claude cannot do, under Try these: each one says what to open, what to do and what you should see, covers one thing only, and says under it why only you can check it (your phone, a real send to a real person, reconnecting an account, or a matter of taste). The plugin refuses a Ready row without those reasons and without Claude's own check written on it. When you tell Claude a row is done, it is recorded as done without asking you for checks.

Each check has what looks like three overlapping buttons: Works and Doesn't work side by side, with Comment below, overlapping the inner lower part of both. That gives five places to press:

  1. Works (the part not overlapped) marks it working at once, and Claude is told at once.
  2. Doesn't work (the part not overlapped) marks it as a fault at once, and Claude is told at once.
  3. Where Comment overlaps Works (the mixed colour), a box opens: "Works, with a comment. What do you want to say?"
  4. Where Comment overlaps Doesn't work, a box opens: "Doesn't work, with a comment. What did you see?"
  5. The part of Comment that overlaps neither opens a box: "Comment only. This check is not marked either way." Your words become a note on the row, and the check stays unmarked.

From any of the three comment places, nothing is written or sent until you tap Done. Clear my mark takes a mark back. When every check on a row is marked, the page shows the row as Done, confirmed (all work) or Ready, known fault (any doesn't), and says "Claude is recording it." Claude then records it properly: for all Works, the row becomes Done, confirmed with "Your checks" and the date added; for a fault, it adds what did not work to the row, gets it fixed and sends you a new check. If you mark Doesn't work with no words, Claude asks what you saw.

Questions

A question that needs a picture or a choice sits on the row it's about, with its pictures and its choices lettered a, b, c. Pick a choice or type in "Anything to add, or your own answer", then tap Answer, and Claude is told. You can change an answer until Claude marks it done. If you answer with a question of your own, Claude answers you and asks again, in clearer words, on the same question; the earlier wording and your reply fold under Earlier on this question. A question Claude has acted on folds under "Earlier questions, already acted on".

Writing on a row

Every row has Say something about this in its open view. Type, tap Done, and Claude is told. Claude acts on it, and the row then shows what changed under What changed. Once it shows that, the note can no longer be changed.

Taking over a roadmap a project already has

If a project already has a roadmap page made some other way, type /assistant:roadmap-setup in that project's main session. Claude finds the page (or asks for its link) and asks once whether to move it onto the plugin. If you say yes, it keeps the same link and everything on it: every row, mark, answer, note and picture. It saves a copy of the database first, shows you what has to change (and asks about anything it cannot settle itself) before writing anything, deletes nothing, writes none of your marks, answers or words, and proves afterwards that every count matches. Rules the old roadmap had that belong only to that project go into a file Project rules.md in the project's Roadmap page folder. If an old Roadmap writer session is still running it is kept and told to follow the plugin's way.

If the project had roadmap locks of its own, Claude never changes them. It gives you two commands to paste in your own PowerShell window: one retires the old locks (it keeps the old settings in the project's Plugin update copy-in folder), the other puts everything back. Until you paste the first, both sets of locks run.

Seeing roadmaps on your page

A project's roadmap shows on your assistant's page once you've said yes to "its roadmap on your page" while introducing that project (setup, or /assistant:introduce later). It appears at once: until the roadmap's writer has made its first summary, it says "Waiting for this roadmap's first summary". Each roadmap is its own part of your page, under its own name; you can move it in Settings (Arranging your page).

  • When the whole roadmap has reached your page, it is shown right in its section, and it looks and works just as it does in Claude: you can mark checks with the three overlapping buttons, answer questions and write on rows. A card's Open it in the roadmap takes you to that section, at the check, row or question. Each press is sent at once and shows "sent, not yet on the roadmap" (at the top of the section, and under the check, row or question) until the roadmap shows it. Then the line clears by itself.
  • Until then, the section shows a short summary: Checks to try, Questions open and when it last changed, "What's next", a table of rows and statuses, and the line "The whole roadmap has not reached this page yet. This is its short summary." Open the full roadmap opens the roadmap page in Claude, where you can mark checks and answer questions.

A press you make on your page is passed to that project's main session at once. The main session hands it to the Roadmap writer (or writes it itself, in a project that writes its own roadmap). Each press is written once. If the main session isn't reachable, the press waits and is handed over at that session's next message. If the roadmap refuses a press, your change is taken back, your typed words are kept, and the page says why.

Stepped and running roadmaps

A roadmap comes in two kinds. Claude asks which one when you set it up, and you can switch later with the roadmap's Settings or by asking Claude.

  • Stepped is the default, for a build with an end. Every step is listed in order, with a strip of numbered squares and counts at the top.
  • Running is for ongoing work that never finishes. The page shows only what is live, in these groups from the top: Now (being built) (being worked on, built and waiting to be merged), Being fixed (ready with a known fault), Waiting on you (checks and questions), Next batch (agreed, queued) and Ideas (talked about, not agreed). Rows are numbered 1, 2, 3 down the page among the live rows and renumbered as they change. There is no strip and no big counters, only one short line of counts. Finished rows move to a History section at the bottom, folded, grouped by release or date (for example "0.1.4, 11/20"); each is one line that opens to its detail.

Checks, questions, marks and notes work the same on every row in both kinds. Claude fills two optional fields on a row to feed a running page: the release a finished row shipped in, and a mark that a planned row is only an idea.

Settings on the roadmap page

Settings, at the bottom of each roadmap page, folds open to that project's settings:

  • Roadmap kind: Stepped or Running.
  • Who writes the roadmap: a Roadmap writer session, or the main session. This one is shown only; the page shows the exact command to run yourself in PowerShell, with Copy (the two lines under "A project that writes its own roadmap").
  • Model helpers use: the cheapest that does the job well (the default), or always the strongest.
  • Questions file: one line for each of the project's sessions, on or off, with Turn on or Turn off.

A press on a choice asks the project's main session to make the change; the button shows "(asked)" and the page keeps showing the old choice until that session has made it, then shows the new one. If it could not, the page says "Not changed:" and why. A questions file is turned on or off only after your yes in that session. Only the page's owner and editors can ask. Until the main session has recorded the settings, the page says "Not recorded yet".

Closing a roadmap down

There are three ways: turn it off in one command, stop it and keep its page by hand, or remove roadmaps completely. None deletes anything except the last step of the third.

Turn a roadmap off in one command

In a session open in the project, type /assistant:roadmap-off (or use Remove roadmap from a project in Settings, Tools). It deletes the project's roadmap records (.claude/roadmap), takes the project out of your assistant's list of roadmaps, and from then on every roadmap check in that project is silent: no reminders and no holds. The published roadmap page stays at its link as a record, and so does the project's Roadmap page folder. Type /reload-plugins afterwards. In a project with no roadmap it says so and changes nothing. The questions file in the same project is not touched. Claude runs it only after you ask for it or say yes in that session. To start again, type /assistant:roadmap-setup.

Stop a roadmap and keep its page

Nothing is deleted this way, and you can bring it back.

  1. Stop the blocks. Claude is never allowed to do this, so you do it in your own Windows PowerShell window (Start menu, type PowerShell, open it). Paste the first line below, with the project's real folder in place of <project folder>, and press Enter. It should answer "The roadmap arrangement is switched off in ...". This puts the project's roadmap settings to one side (as .claude\roadmap-off), so the blocks, the reminders and the lock in that project all stand down. Nothing else in the project changes.
  2. End the Roadmap writer session. In the Claude app, find that project's session called Roadmap writer in your list of sessions and archive it. The plugin has no step of its own for this.
  3. Stop showing it on your page. If your assistant shows this roadmap, type /assistant:introduce in your assistant's folder and choose 3, remove one. It asks about each part separately. Say yes to "Stop showing its roadmap on your page", and no to any part you want to keep; the project can stay introduced.
  4. Leave the page. The roadmap's page on claude.ai keeps its link and everything on it (rows, marks, answers, notes, pictures). It just stops changing. It's private unless you shared it.

To bring it back, paste the second line below the same way. Then, in a Code tab session in that project, type /assistant:roadmap-setup to make that session the main session, and /assistant:roadmap-start-writer if no Roadmap writer is running.

The path is the assistant's own copy of its roadmap program. It's there once you've used the plugin in any Code tab session.

Remove roadmaps completely

Do this when you want a project to have no roadmap at all, for example to start a new one from scratch later. Only the last step can't be undone.

  1. For each project, turn the roadmap off (/assistant:roadmap-off, or steps 1 to 3 above), end the Roadmap writer session, and stop showing it on your page.
  2. Delete the project's roadmap files. In the project's folder: .claude\roadmap-off (only if you used the by-hand way; /assistant:roadmap-off already deleted .claude/roadmap), Roadmap page (the project's copy of the page, its summaries and its own roadmap rules), and, if the roadmap was taken over from an older one, Plugin update copy-in. You can delete them in File Explorer (the .claude folder is hidden: View, Show, Hidden items), or ask Claude in a session in that project. Once the roadmap is switched off, Claude is allowed to delete them.
  3. Check for old locks. The plugin's own blocks live inside the plugin and leave nothing behind in the project. A project that had roadmap locks of its own from before the plugin still has lines for them in its .claude\settings.json or .claude\settings.local.json, and scripts in its .claude\hooks folder. Take those out by hand, or ask Claude in that project to show them and take them out after your yes. If those old locks refuse Claude, do it by hand.
  4. If you used the old, separate Roadmap plugin, take it off completely: type the uninstall line from part 3 ("Switching from an older setup") in every project it was installed on, or once if it was installed for all of Claude, then remove the place Claude installed it from with the line below (roadmap-plugin is the name after the @ in its install name; use yours if it differs), and close the Claude app completely and open it again.
  5. Delete the page itself, if you want to. It can't be undone. Ask Claude in any Code tab session to delete the artifact at the page's link; Claude asks you to confirm first.

The roadmap part of the assistant plugin stays installed, but it does nothing in a project with no roadmap. To start a new roadmap there later, type /assistant:roadmap-setup and you get a fresh, empty page.

Keeping a project up to date

After you update the plugin (part 6), each project with a roadmap needs bringing up to the new version. The update offers to send each roadmap's main session this step; or, in that project's main session, type /assistant:roadmap-update. It puts the new page at the same link with the same title, changes no row, mark, answer or note, reloads the main session's and the writer's instructions, and gives you a numbered list of what changed for you. If nothing is behind, it says "Nothing to update" and does nothing. It runs only after you ask for it or say yes in that session.

The questions file

In a long discussion, a question Claude asks you can scroll out of sight and never get answered, or an answer like "1a" can belong to more than one question. The questions file keeps them in one place: a file at the top of the project's folder, one per session, holding only the questions that session raised and that are still open, oldest first, numbered from 1 the way numbered questions look in chat (the question, then its lettered choices, what Claude recommends and the date asked indented beneath it).

  1. In the Claude app's Code tab, start a session in the project's folder and type /assistant:questions-setup (or use Turn on in the roadmap's Settings, or Add questions file to a project). That session keeps its own file: Questions.md if no session in the project has it, otherwise Questions - <the session's title>.md. It makes the file ("No open questions.") if it isn't there, and opens it in the side panel beside the chat (outside the Claude app, it gives you a link to the file).
  2. From then on, every question it puts to you in discussion is written into the file first. You answer in chat, by number and letter ("2b") or in your own words.
  3. It replies to each answer with one line naming the question in words ("Took 2b as: ..."), takes the question out of the file and renumbers the rest, so number 1 is always the oldest. It doesn't list the open questions in chat; the file is the list. An answer that asks for more keeps the question open, reworded, in its place. A question it no longer needs is dropped with a one-line note. You read the file and never edit it.

Two checks make it stick. On each message, and when a session starts or resumes, that session's own open questions are put in front of it. It has its reply held once, with a plain reason, if you answered and its file didn't change, or if the reply asks you a question that isn't in the file; after three holds in a row it's let go. Other sessions in the project, forks and sessions started from it included, don't share the file and get nothing from these checks unless they turn on their own; a helper or fork with no file just asks in chat.

To turn it off, type /assistant:questions-off in the session (or Turn off in the roadmap's Settings, or Remove questions file from a project). The checks stop for that session only, its file stays as it is (nothing deleted) and other sessions' files are untouched. A session that never turned it on is left alone. Both commands run only after you ask for them or say yes in that session.

Using both in one project

A project can have a roadmap and a questions file at once. Where a question goes depends on where it came from: a question raised by the work goes on the roadmap, on the row it is about; a question raised in conversation goes in the questions file. A question goes in one place, never both. In a project with only one of the two, every question to you goes there; with neither, it is asked in the chat. Each tool has its own reminders and checks, so turning one on or off does not change the other. You answer roadmap questions by tapping on the roadmap page, and conversation questions by number and letter in the chat. When the session tells you it put a work question on the roadmap, or repeats an answer you gave there, the questions file's check leaves that alone.

6. Updating and uninstalling Contents

Updating

Nothing is updated without your say-so. Once a week, on Sunday just after midnight, your assistant looks for a newer version by comparing version numbers (it doesn't use Claude for this, and with no internet it simply tries again later). When there is one, a card appears on your page: "A newer version of your assistant is ready", with a list of what it brings. Its Update button starts a walk-through in Talk to your assistant at the top of your page. You can also press Start beside Update in Settings, Tools, or type /assistant:update at your computer.

The walk-through says what the update brings, then goes through each change you made yourself to how your assistant works and shows how the update would touch it. For each one it asks, in plain words:

  • your change fits beside the update: 1 keep it, joined with the update, 2 drop it;
  • the update now does the same thing itself: 1 use the built-in one (suggested), 2 keep your own;
  • the update changes the very same lines: 1 take the update's, 2 keep yours, 3 combine them (you see the result before it is used).

It also checks your own changes against your assistant's rules and says if one would now break a rule. Then it says everything back and asks "Shall I put the update in now? 1 yes, 2 not now." Nothing changes before that yes.

The last part restarts your assistant, so it is finished at your computer. If you started on the page, Claude tells you your choices are saved and to type /assistant:update; it carries on from there. At the computer:

  1. Type /assistant:update in the Code tab, in your assistant's folder. Claude fetches the newest plugin. If it fetched a newer one, quit the Claude app completely (right-click its icon near the clock, Quit), open it again, and type /assistant:update once more.
  2. Claude goes through the walk-through above, or, if you did it on your page, says back what you chose and asks for the yes again.
  3. It saves the old copy and your data, writes the new version together with the changes of yours you kept, and checks that the program loads and your page answers. If any check fails it puts everything back by itself and tells you which step failed; the update card stays so you can try again. It runs no reading pass of its own.
  4. If your assistant is in the middle of its regular check when the update starts, the update waits for it, up to 10 minutes. If it is still running after that, nothing is changed and you are told to run the update again once the check is done.
  5. If it says "Nothing to update", your copy already matches the plugin file for file. The same version number with files that differ still counts as an update, so a change made to the plugin on this computer reaches your assistant at the next /assistant:update.

Your other sessions. After an update, every other Claude session open on your computer needs reloading to have the new commands and checks, and a project with a roadmap also needs its roadmap brought up to the new version. Claude lists those sessions, one line each with what it needs, and asks: 1 send each its step, 2 pick which (for example "2 1,3"), 3 none. Nothing is sent until you answer. A session that gets its step shows you one line, /reload-plugins, which only you can type there; a roadmap's main session then updates its roadmap itself and reports back. A session you skip can do its step later: type /reload-plugins in it, and in a roadmap's main session /assistant:roadmap-update. Outside the Claude app, where it cannot see your sessions, it lists the projects with a roadmap or a questions file instead and says what to type in each.

Trying the page with test cards. Ask Claude in your assistant's folder to add test cards: a set of made-up cards, each titled TEST:, goes on your page. A press on one changes only that card: nothing is sent, and no account, junk list or rule is touched. Ask Claude to remove the test cards when you are done; they leave nothing behind.

Uninstalling

  1. In the Claude app's Code tab, type /assistant:uninstall.
  2. For each project where an introduction made a change, Claude asks "Take your assistant's things out of that project too? 1 yes, 2 leave them" and undoes the line in its instructions, the introduction and its roadmap on your page only on a yes. It tells you what stays in a project you leave.
  3. Choose 1 to remove the program and keep your data (your rules, history and sign-ins stay in the Live folder), or 2 to remove everything, your data too. Removing data can't be undone.
  4. Claude stops the background job and anything running, switches off reaching your page from anywhere, and removes the program.
  5. Claude shows that no job, nothing running and no program are left.
  6. The last step is yours: type /plugin uninstall assistant@assistant, then quit and reopen the Claude app.

Roadmaps already set up in projects keep their pages. Their blocks stop when the plugin is gone.
